\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Supervised change detection.} Siamese encoders and their transformer
successors dominate benchmark tables: FC-Siam-Diff~\cite{daudt2018fully},
STANet~\cite{chen2020spatial}, SNUNet~\cite{fang2021snunet},
BIT~\cite{chen2021remote}, ChangeFormer~\cite{bandara2022transformer} and
TinyCD~\cite{codegoni2023tinycd} reach 86--91 F1 on LEVIR-CD. They are trained
on that dataset's own training split and predict a binary mask. Neither
property suits a service that must name and count objects in a neighbourhood it
was never trained on, and we print these methods as a reference ceiling rather
than as competitors.

\textbf{Open-vocabulary and zero-shot change detection.} AnyChange
\cite{zheng2024segment} adapts SAM to change detection without training on the
target dataset, reaching \AnyChangeFOne{} F1 on LEVIR-CD and \AnyChangeHFOne{}
in a human-assisted three-point query setting. This is our league. Our result
sits in a third position that we state plainly rather than blur: the detector
has never seen LEVIR-CD, but the pairing head is trained on its training split
from mask-derived labels, so the LEVIR-CD number is not zero-shot. The WHU-CD
experiment, where that same head is applied without retraining, is what carries
the zero-shot claim.

\textbf{Change captioning.} RSICC-style models
\cite{hoxha2022change,liu2022remote,liu2023progressive,chang2023changes}
generate a sentence per image pair and are scored with BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L
and CIDEr-D against human references. These
are trained on the caption corpus, and n-gram overlap rewards phrasing that
matches the reference distribution. Our concern is orthogonal: a report can
score well on overlap while asserting a change that did not occur, and we show
a case where it does.

\textbf{Grounding and hallucination.} Retrieval-augmented generation reduces
unsupported assertions by conditioning on retrieved evidence, and graph-based
variants aggregate over entities and communities rather than top-$k$ passages.
Evaluating the result is the harder half. Metrics that use a language model as
judge inherit its failure modes and can be gamed by the model under test. We
take the opposite approach: a deterministic rule-based extractor reduces a
report to atomic claims, and those claims are checked against human references
and ground-truth masks. Nothing in the metric is learned, so a model cannot
optimise against the judge.
