\section{Introduction}

Consider the product this paper is built for. A homeowner watching a plot next
to theirs, a small builder tracking three sites, someone deciding whether the
district they are about to move into is being built up or emptied out. Each
pins an area in an app. After every satellite revisit they receive a short
message: \emph{four new buildings on the north edge; the lot behind the school
has been cleared}. Nobody in this audience wants a segmentation mask, and
nobody will open one.

Two things made this a consumer proposition rather than an institutional one.
Revisit intervals over populated areas have fallen to the point where a given
neighbourhood is re-imaged on a cadence that supports a subscription rather
than a commissioned study, and the per-request cost of imagery and inference
has fallen with it. What was an analyst workflow becomes an app that has to run
unattended, for many subscribers, at a cost per notification that a consumer
price point can absorb.

That shift changes which errors matter, and it changes them in a way that
segmentation metrics cannot express. The deliverable is a sentence, and a
sentence makes commitments a mask does not. It says \emph{how many} buildings
appeared. It says \emph{whether} anything appeared at all. Two product failures
follow.

The first is invention. A report that confidently announces construction that
never happened is worse than no report: the subscriber walks out to look, finds
nothing, and stops trusting the service. This failure is invisible to
pixel-level precision, which is computed against a mask the user never sees.

The second is miscounting. ``Several buildings appeared'' when the answer is
one, or forty when the answer is four, is directionally correct and
operationally useless. A change-detection model can score well on IoU while the
instance count derived from it is off by an order of magnitude, because IoU
rewards covering the right pixels rather than resolving the right objects.

Both failures are what a subscriber would file a complaint about, and neither
appears in the metrics the field optimises.

\textbf{The obvious build fails on both.} A practitioner asked to ship this
today would hand both images to a capable vision--language model and ask for a
description. We measure that baseline rather than dismiss it, and it fails in
the way that is hardest to notice: it writes fluent, well-formed, confident
prose while deciding at chance (\EFourVLMChgAcc\%) whether the scene changed at
all. Its BLEU-4 is \EFourVLMBleu, which a practitioner would read as a
stylistic mismatch rather than as the symptom it is. A service built this way
would look correct in demos and be wrong half the time in production.

\textbf{Our approach} grounds the text in explicit instance evidence, using the
current AI stack end to end. A promptable segmentation foundation model
(\DetectorName)~\cite{sam3} proposes object instances in each frame
open-vocabulary, without having been trained on either benchmark;
CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} embeddings describe each instance's appearance;
a learned head decides which detections across revisits are the same physical
object and which unmatched detections are genuine change; surviving instances
accumulate in a persistent spatio-temporal knowledge graph keyed by location,
so a pinned neighbourhood carries its own history forward; and a large language
model (\LLMName)~\cite{exaone4} writes the report from that structure rather
than from pixels. Because the report is
assembled from objects, counting is a property of the evidence rather than of
the prose.

\textbf{The result that surprised us concerns where in this stack factuality is
won.} The natural assumption --- and the one the retrieval-augmented generation
literature encourages --- is that the language model's context is the lever:
give it more history, aggregate that history better, and it will assert fewer
things that are not true. Over \EFourNCrops{} crops we find otherwise.
Aggregating raw detections into a change inventory is worth
\EFourAggGain{} CFS-F1, but adding top-$k$ retrieval of past observations
on top of that, or replacing it with graph entity and community aggregation,
moves factuality by less than \EFourLadderSpread{} points --- in both
directions. What does move it, by \EFiveGain{} points, is improving the pairing
that produced the evidence in the first place. For a CE system built on this
stack the design implication is direct: spend the engineering budget upstream of
the language model, not on its prompt.

The accuracy lives in a component small enough to be an implementation detail:
a \HeadParamsRound-parameter model, supervised for free from the change masks
that public benchmarks already ship. It also addresses an error mode specific
to this design. If the segmenter misses a building in the earlier frame and
finds it in the later one, pairing has nothing to pair against and the object
is reported as new construction --- the invention failure, arriving through the
detector rather than the language model. We compute cross-frame co-located
evidence, comparing each detection's footprint against the same coordinates in
the other frame whether or not the detector fired there, and hand it to a
learned verifier.

\textbf{Cost is a first-class constraint here, not an afterthought.} A
monitoring service pays for every tile of every subscriber on every revisit, so
an accuracy gain that doubles inference cost does not ship. We measure the
per-stage budget and show the learned head adds \ESixHeadDelta~ms per tile to a
pipeline that spends \ESixDetectShare\% of its time in segmentation ---
\ESixPipelineShare\% of the total, which is what makes the accuracy free in
practice.

Our contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An LLM change-reporting pipeline built on segmentation and
      vision--language foundation models}, with a persistent spatio-temporal
      knowledge graph for neighbourhood history, evaluated on the factuality of
      its prose rather than only on segmentation overlap.
\item \textbf{Where factuality is won in such a pipeline.} Over
      \EFourNCrops{} crops, retrieval and graph aggregation over the same
      evidence move CFS-F1 by less than \EFourLadderSpread{} points, while
      improving the upstream pairing moves it by \EFiveGain{} and drops
      unsupported claims from \EFiveHeuristicHal\% to \EFiveLearnedHal\% --- at
      \ESixHeadDelta~ms per tile, \ESixPipelineShare\% of the pipeline. The
      lever is the evidence, not the language model's context.
\item \textbf{Learned instance pairing, and the finding that motivates it.}
      Every pairing strategy we tested that does \emph{not} learn ---
      geometry-only, a hand-tuned CLIP-plus-geometry heuristic, and the
      strongest published zero-shot method --- lands in the same narrow band
      (\EOneGeoFOne, \EOneHeuristicFOne{} and \AnyChangeFOne{} F1) at precision
      13--16, regardless of segmentation quality. A
      \HeadParamsRound-parameter head, supervised for free from the change
      masks benchmarks already publish, leaves that band: \EOnePixelFOne{}
      pixel F1 on LEVIR-CD. It includes cross-frame co-located evidence, which
      addresses the failure that pairing alone cannot --- an object the
      detector missed in the earlier frame.
\item \textbf{Transfer without retraining.} The same head, unchanged and with
      thresholds carried over, reaches \ETwoPixelFOne{} on WHU-CD against
      \ETwoHeuristicFOne{} for the heuristic it replaces, and cuts instance
      count error from \ETwoHeuristicCountMAE{} to \ETwoCountMAE{} --- the
      difference between a subscriber-facing count that is usable and one that
      is not.
\item \textbf{Change-Fact-Score, and the failure it exposes.} A claim-level
      factuality metric with no language model inside it, so the system under
      evaluation cannot optimise against the judge. It shows that a capable
      vision--language model shown both images writes fluent prose while
      deciding at chance (\EFourVLMChgAcc\%) whether the scene changed at all
      --- a failure n-gram overlap registers only faintly.
\end{itemize}

Two further results constrain how such systems should be evaluated rather than
advancing our own, and we state them because the alternative is to present a
null as a gain. A crop-level grounding ladder \emph{cannot} measure a knowledge
graph's contribution, for a structural reason we give in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; and on our data a deterministic template is
indistinguishable from a language model on factuality, so the case for
generation rests on readability, which we do not measure.

We are explicit about scope. We evaluate on public benchmarks, not on a live
subscriber base; the consumer setting motivates the design and the choice of
metrics rather than supplying the evaluation data. We do not claim
state-of-the-art pixel-level change detection --- models trained on these
benchmarks' own training splits reach 86--91 F1 and we do not approach that.
The comparisons we make are against methods that have not been trained on the
benchmark, and against the build a practitioner would try first.
