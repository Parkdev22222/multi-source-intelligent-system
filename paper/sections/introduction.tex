\section{Introduction}

We consider a consumer satellite-monitoring service in which users select an
area and receive a short written report after each new observation. Unlike
conventional change-detection systems that produce pixel-level masks for expert
analysis, this service must describe detected changes as accessible object-level
statements, such as ``four new buildings appeared on the northern edge'' or
``the lot behind the school was cleared.''

This shift changes which errors matter in ways that conventional segmentation
metrics cannot express. The deliverable is a sentence, and a sentence makes commitments a mask
does not: it says \emph{how many} buildings appeared, and \emph{whether}
anything appeared at all. Two product failures follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Invention.} A report announcing construction that never happened
      is worse than no report: the subscriber walks out to look, finds nothing,
      and stops trusting the service. Pixel precision cannot see this, because
      it is computed against a mask the user never sees.
\item \textbf{Miscounting.} A report may detect new construction correctly but
      claim that several buildings appeared when only one did, undermining its
      reliability for operational monitoring. Pixel-level IoU may remain high even when the model predicts the wrong
      number of buildings, because IoU measures spatial overlap rather than
      instance separation or counting accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{A direct vision--language baseline fails on both.} A practitioner asked to ship this
today would hand both images to a vision--language model and ask for a
description. We measure that baseline rather than dismiss it: it writes fluent,
confident prose while reaching only \EFourVLMChgAcc\% accuracy on whether the
scene changed. Its BLEU-4 is
\EFourVLMBleu, which reads as a stylistic mismatch rather than as the symptom
it is. Such a service would look correct in demos while remaining unreliable
in many evaluated cases.

\textbf{Our approach} generates reports from object instances detected and
matched across two images. \DetectorName~\cite{sam3} generates object-instance
proposals using open-vocabulary text prompts, and CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning}
encodes their appearance. A lightweight pairing head then matches proposals
across revisits and verifies whether unmatched instances represent genuine
changes. The resulting object-level evidence, augmented with location-based
graph context, is provided to \LLMName~\cite{exaone4} for report generation.
Consequently, building counts are derived from explicit object instances rather
than unconstrained language generation.

\textbf{Pairing is the report-level bottleneck.} Holding the report model,
prompt and graph grounding fixed, replacing only the pairing increases
Change-Fact-Score (CFS), the deterministic claim-level metric defined in
Section~\ref{ssec:cfs}, by \EFiveGain{} F1 points and lowers unsupported
claims from
\EFiveHeuristicHal\% to \EFiveLearnedHal\%. With instance evidence fixed,
structured reporting, top-$k$ retrieval, and graph aggregation achieve CFS-F1
scores of \EFourStructFOne{}, \EFourFlatRagFOne{}, and \EFourGraphRAGFOne{},
respectively, on \EFourNCrops{} crops---a maximum difference of only
\EFourLadderExactSpread{} points. The central result is therefore not that a
larger context makes the writer safer, but that better correspondence makes its
evidence reliable.

Our contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Learned instance pairing.} Every non-learned strategy we tested
      --- geometry, a hand-tuned CLIP-plus-geometry heuristic, and the
      published zero-shot reference --- lands in the same narrow band
      (\EOneGeoFOne, \EOneHeuristicFOne, \AnyChangeFOne{} F1) regardless of
      segmentation quality, although the supervision settings differ. A
      \HeadParamsRound-parameter head trained without additional correspondence
      annotations leaves it at
      \EOnePixelFOne{} and transfers to WHU-CD unchanged at \ETwoPixelFOne,
      cutting instance count error from \ETwoHeuristicCountMAE{} to
      \ETwoCountMAE.
\item \textbf{Detection-to-report evidence.} That gain is measured with the
      generation and the grounding held fixed, at \ESixHeadDelta~ms additional
      latency per tile.
\item \textbf{Deterministic report evaluation.} We use a domain-specific
      protocol for claim direction and object class, with CountMAE for counts
      and no language model inside the evaluator.
\item \textbf{Grounding analysis.} Retrieval and graph aggregation over fixed
      crop-level evidence show only a \EFourLadderSpread-point observed spread;
      graph aggregation instead contributes to neighborhood-level count
      preservation.
\end{itemize}

We are explicit about scope. We evaluate on public benchmarks, not a live
subscriber base; the consumer setting motivates the design and the metrics
rather than supplying the data. We do not claim state-of-the-art pixel-level
change detection --- models trained on these benchmarks' own splits reach
86--91 F1 and we do not approach that. AnyChange supplies zero-shot context
rather than a supervision-matched comparison; our controlled baselines are the
pairing strategies evaluated with the same detections and data.
