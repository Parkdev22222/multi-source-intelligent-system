\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Where Report Factuality Is Determined}

The controlled pairing swap provides the main system result, and its
\EFiveGain-point CFS-F1 gain enters before generation: the language model
cannot recover an object that was incorrectly paired or remove a false change
already admitted to its inventory.

The knowledge graph answers a narrower question (Section~\ref{ssec:ablation}).
At crop level every condition receives the same current-crop inventory, so
retrieval and graph aggregation have little opportunity to change a scored
claim. At neighborhood level the representations genuinely differ, and graph
aggregation preserves counts better than concatenation or top-$k$ retrieval.
This is a counting result, not evidence that GraphRAG improves factuality.

A second null belongs here for the same reason. On our data a deterministic
template is indistinguishable from a language model on CFS, and at
neighborhood level it leads on CFS-F1. The case for generation rests on
readability and on producing a count a reader can act on; we measure only the
second.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{We do not approach the supervised ceiling.} End-to-end models trained
on these splits reach 86--91 F1 on LEVIR-CD; we reach \EOnePixelFOne. AnyChange
is zero-shot context, not a supervision-matched baseline, since our head uses
LEVIR-CD training masks.

\textbf{The LEVIR-CD result is not zero-shot.} The detector never saw the
dataset, but the head was trained on its training split from mask-derived
labels. The WHU-CD transfer carries the zero-shot claim.

\textbf{The temporal half of the graph is untested.} The graph is designed to
accumulate entity histories across many revisits, but both benchmarks carry
exactly two timepoints, so what we exercise is spatial aggregation over the
crops of one tile, not history over time. The design's main hypothesis is
therefore not evaluated here.

\textbf{Claim extraction is rule-based and not independently validated.} This
keeps a language model out of the metric, but the extractor sees only the cues
it encodes and is tuned to this domain's vocabulary. Agreement with independently
annotated claims should be established before treating CFS as general.

\textbf{CFS lacks an independently generated specialist baseline.} Published
change-captioning outputs were unavailable in a form we could rescore, so all
CFS rows use outputs from our harness. Conventional caption metrics give
published context but do not validate the new metric.

\textbf{Generation uncertainty is not quantified.} Each condition is one
generation pass, so the small crop-level differences among structured,
flat-retrieval and graph conditions are descriptive, not significant effects.

\textbf{Both benchmarks are building-centric, aerial rather than satellite,
and already aligned}, so generalization to other change types, to lower ground
sampling distances, and to acquisitions that are not is untested ---
cross-frame evidence compares the same footprint in both frames, so
misalignment degrades it.

\textbf{The report model is licensed for research use only.} \LLMName{} is
released under a non-commercial license, so the service this paper is written
around would swap in a commercially licensed model. Nothing in the pipeline
depends on which model writes the report, but nothing here shows the factuality
numbers survive that swap either.

\subsection{Future Work}

The experiment this work now needs is a dataset with a real revisit history:
more than two acquisitions of the same area, which would test the temporal
aggregation the graph was built for and which neither benchmark provides.
Beyond that, three gaps remain between this evaluation and a live deployment:
change types other than buildings, true satellite ground sampling distances,
and a commercially licensed model. Independent validation of the claim extractor
and paired multi-seed tests are the immediate priorities.
